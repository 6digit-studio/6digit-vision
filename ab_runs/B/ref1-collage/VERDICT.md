# Verdict

## Sensing mode
Vision-first over `reference.png`, combined with the precomputed `facts.json` sheet, and a small number of pixel samples of the reference used only to calibrate geometry, text sizes and gradient stops (the `eyes` tool itself was not run).

## What is missing
- **Card 4 orb (largest gap).** The photographic plasma/quantum-processor texture is approximated with SVG ellipses, turbulence and displacement. Mine reads as a dark-purple sphere with a uniform neon-magenta rim; the reference is a warm iridescent glassy sphere with orange/yellow fringing, a white-pink rosette across most of the disc and a black cavity on the left. My cast shadow also forms a visible maroon halo above and around the orb where the reference background stays clean light gray, and the dawn gradient behind it therefore starts/reads too low.
- **Card 1 orb.** Same texture limitation: the reference has a busy pink/white fleshy rosette with strong chromatic fringes and a steel-blue wedge; my version is smoother, darker, and carries a bright pink/blue ring rather than the reference's thin iridescent edge.
- **Card 3 sphere.** The reference has a crisp fan-shaped specular reflection at the upper left, a distinctly darker lower-left body and a soft blue glow; mine is a smoother radial gradient with a much softer highlight and slightly weaker dark underside.
- **Logo wordmark.** "KODOR" is set in system Helvetica/SF Bold with tracking, but the reference uses a custom geometric sans (different K, O, D and R shapes); the mark's double chevron is close but not a match, and overall weight is slightly different.
- **Card 2 paragraph fade.** The reference's paragraph lightens continuously from about `#5f5f5f` to `#dedede` down the block; I approximated it with per-line colours, so the gradient is stepwise rather than continuous (line positions and widths match within 1–2 px).

## Self-estimate
6/10 — the four-card layout, card geometry (328x696 at x=53/415/777/1139, y=60, 27 px radius), type sizes, line positions, KODOR lockups, both dawn gradients and the light/dark sectioning are very close, but the photographic orb/sphere artwork and the card-4 halo are visibly different from the reference and occupy a large share of the image.
